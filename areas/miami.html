Mainland Miami, not the Beach

Miami on this page means the city side of the bay: downtown, Brickell, the Design District, Wynwood, Coral Gables, Coconut Grove, and the neighborhoods inland of the water. Miami Beach has its own page because that work is mostly hotel guest rooms and oceanfront glass. The products overlap. The buildings do not.

Mainland glass has its own problems. Brickell living rooms look across other towers. Grove houses take a hard slice of sun on one side and deep shade on another. A Design District storefront wants a clear view of the sidewalk in the afternoon and privacy after dark. One product does not cover those openings. Roller shades, drapery, motorized shades, and zip screens are all things we measure in the city. The right one depends on the opening.

Condos, dining rooms, and treatment rooms

Condos are a large part of Miami work. The window is often already impact glass. Impact glass is not a shade. It still needs something for glare and for evening privacy. We measure the opening that is there, including frame depth, so a shade or a panel does not collide with a crank or a slider.

A dining room may want sheers plus a heavier drape, which is what Osteria Morini uses on Lincoln Road. That restaurant is on Miami Beach, not in Brickell. A treatment room may want blackout drapery, which is the Adhara Spa install. Those projects show the cloth. They are not a claim that both buildings sit on the mainland. For a Miami address, we come to that address and price from those measurements.

Courtyards and roof decks are the zip-screen jobs: a tracked outdoor shade, separate from the roller shade on the glass. Fort Lauderdale patios and Boca and Palm Beach houses are the same idea in other cities.

Questions

Do impact windows replace shades in a Miami condo?

No. Impact glass is a window. Glare and evening privacy still need a shade or drapery, measured to that frame.

Is this page for Miami Beach hotels?

No. Beach hotels are on the Miami Beach page. This page is for mainland Miami: downtown, Brickell, the Grove, Gables, and the Design District.

How do I get an estimate in Miami?

Call, email, or use the estimate form. We come to the address. Measuring is free, and the price depends on the measurements.
