What a zip screen is

A zip screen is an outdoor shade. The fabric travels in tracks on both sides, so the cloth stays in the opening instead of blowing free the way a loose curtain would. People use them on patios, pergolas, and the open sides of restaurants when they want shade, fewer insects, or a break from a light breeze without building a solid wall.

South Florida spends much of the year outside. A Fort Lauderdale terrace, a Miami Beach hotel deck, and a Palm Beach pergola all hit the same limit: the sun is the point of the seating until it is too much. A zip screen drops into that opening and rolls away when you want the air. It is not a window roller shade, and it is not a storm shutter. Window shades live on glass. Zip screens live on outdoor frames.

Options

Fabric is about view and shade. A darker screen cloth cuts glare and still lets you see the yard or the sidewalk. A more opaque cloth gives privacy and a deeper shade. We look at the frame, the width, and what the opening has to do. A restaurant that resets between lunch and dinner may want screens staff can move together. A house pergola may need one bay.

These screens are motorized. The control is a remote or a switch at the opening, chosen when we measure, in the same spirit as an indoor motorized shade. We do not publish a wind rating here. Exposure changes by patio. The visit is where we see the mounting surface and how the screen will be used.

Questions

Is a zip screen the same as a roller shade?

No. A roller shade covers a window. A zip screen covers an outdoor opening and rides in side tracks.

Can a restaurant use zip screens on an open dining side?

Yes. That is a common use. We measure the opening and price from that size, the fabric, and the motor.

Do zip screens replace storm shutters?

No. They are for daily shade, insects, and breeze. They are not storm shutters.

How do we get a price?

We come measure. The estimate is free. The price depends on the measurements, fabric, and motor. There is no flat patio price.
